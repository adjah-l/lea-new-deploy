Years impact

Family Dinner Foundation started as simple grassroots gatherings at the dinner table. Over ten years, it grew into a movement of 50,000+ adults across 40 cities and three continents. When the pandemic hit, the gatherings stopped, but the relationships did not. This season forced an evolution, developing the 5C Mutual Care Methodology, now successfully piloted on university campuses and licensed within healthcare institutions.

But demand soon outpaced grassroots delivery. Seeking to scale this culture of care, we launched a Social Infrastructure Technology Studio as an independent entity under the Foundation. I learned to code and built the products myself, testing them within the communities we already serve. We are now operating in a closing window: awareness of the loneliness crisis is at its peak, and institutional demand is rising.

All four products in our portfolio are currently in public beta. We are raising runway for the next 12 to 18 months to transition from beta to full market distribution and capture this unique opportunity to build the world's relational infrastructure.

“How do we use technology as a tool for human flourishing, rather than resigning ourselves to a reality where humans have become tools of technology?”
The structural pivot

Under the 501(c)(3) Foundation, our Technology Studio bridges the gap between human need and scalable tools. This structure gives partners a path to contribute to meaningful structural impact with real tax value. We are moving from grassroots gatherings to technology as a tool for human flourishing.
